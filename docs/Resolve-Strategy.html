The upgraded solution, answer by answer

User: the AP lead at a multi-site UAE buyer with ≥ AED 50M revenue, such as a hospital group, retail/F&B chain or distributor. These businesses have many receiving points and many price changes.

Pain: the invoice matches its format but can't be paid because evidence is missing. A goods receipt wasn't posted at a branch, or a price change was never approved in procurement. AP becomes a switchboard, chasing people by email and WhatsApp.

Why the existing tools miss it: matching engines detect the mismatch. Nobody owns closing it, because the missing evidence sits outside AP.

Illustrative size (assumptions): 6,000 invoices/month × 18.4% exception rate (Ardent avg) ≈ 1,100 exceptions/month. If Resolve saves 12 min each, that is ≈ 220 hours/month, or ≈ AED 16,500 of capacity at AED 75/h. Test price: AED 6,000/month plus onboarding. Earlier payment also means fewer supplier disputes and price holds. The pilot measures the real values.

  1. Intake. A structured invoice arrives from the ASP or ERP (OCR only for legacy invoices). Invoices are idempotent by content hash.
  2. Prove. Deterministic decimal rules allocate receipts line by line and split the gap into owned blockers. R-1042: 100 boxes at AED 22 billed, 80 received at AED 20 → AED 400 quantity blocker (receiving) + AED 200 price blocker (procurement).
  3. Investigate. The agent reads case-scoped sources and drafts the smallest set of targeted requests, one per owner, each with a deadline and the exact record it needs back.
  4. Approve and send. A human approves the recipients and the wording.
  5. Re-prove. Only authoritative records clear blockers. A posted GRN clears the AED 400; a supplier email clears nothing. An approved amendment clears the AED 200.
  6. Close. If the evidence supports full payment, Resolve sends an evidence-complete packet to the ERP approval flow. If not, it drafts a PINT AE credit-note request: lines, amount, proposed reason code, linked to the original invoice. AP confirms it, the supplier issues it through its ASP, and Resolve verifies the credit note that comes back.

Rule: the model investigates, code proves, a human authorises, the ERP pays.

  • Evidence store: immutable records with source ID, version, hash, trust level, retrieval time and field/page provenance.
  • Rules engine: decimal math, line-level receipt allocation (one delivery can't clear two invoices), tolerance, duplicate billing, and abstention on stale data.
  • Investigation agent: one structured-output model, six typed case-scoped tools, a call/cost budget. Its job is the messy part: reading Arabic/English emails, delivery notes and photos into candidate evidence, working out who owns what, drafting requests, and summarising for the reviewer. Candidate evidence never clears a blocker by itself.
  • Policy gateway (in code): rejects unknown source IDs, wrong owners, cross-case references and unsupported actions. Pay and bank-change tools do not exist.
  • Stack: Python/FastAPI, Postgres, React, private object storage, background jobs. No fine-tuning, no vector DB in year one.
  • Day 1 (read-only exports): PO lines, GRNs, invoices, credit notes, vendor master, approval matrix, plus email threads for the sampled cases.
  • Pilot: read-only ERP API (SAP B1/S4, Oracle, Dynamics, Odoo, Zoho), an AP mailbox, and the buyer's ASP feed for PINT AE invoices and credit notes.
  • Write-back (after pilot, gated): case status and the packet into ERP workflow, credit-note requests to the supplier. No payment fields, ever.
  • Data residency: UAE-hosted tenant. Supplier content is stored as untrusted input.

  • Labels from outcomes: the ERP history already records how each blocked invoice was resolved (a GRN posted later, an amendment, a credit note). That outcome is the label. Finance only checks 30 cases instead of labelling 150.
  • Split: 150 historical blocked invoices, 50 to build on, 100 held out, plus a separate adversarial set (duplicate deliveries, stale records, injected instructions, look-alike suppliers).
  • Gates: ≥95% recall and ≥90% precision on in-scope blockers, 0 critical false clears, 0 unauthorised side effects. Results reported per exception type, with abstentions counted.
  • Ablation: the same flow with rules + templates. If the model doesn't beat the templates on time-to-resolution and request quality, it doesn't ship.
  • Shadow mode: run on the live queue without sending anything, and compare against what the AP team actually did.

New 45-second opening

"In the UAE, about two in five B2B invoices get paid late. Cash is the main reason, but two of the top four are approval delays and goods not as agreed. Those aren't cash problems. Nobody can prove those invoices are right.

Freehand just raised $75M to let AI decide whether to pay. I'd build the Gulf version the other way round: the AI does the proving and the controller keeps the authority. That limit isn't in a prompt. Resolve has no tool that can pay.

From 1 January 2027, every large UAE invoice is structured data, and every partial dispute must end in a credit note with a reason code. Resolve takes a blocked invoice to one of two endings: evidence-complete, or a ready-to-file credit-note request. It's running. Let me show you R-1042."

Open decisions and risks

Name. "Resolve" collides with Resolve Pay (US B2B net-terms fintech that markets "AI agents run your AR"). Option: Bayyina (بيّنة, "clear proof"), from the legal maxim that the burden of proof is on the claimant, which fits an invoice exactly. Small existing users of the name: an offline law-office tool and a 19-follower consultancy. Needs a trademark check.

Reason-code mapping is an interpretation. Short delivery or a price cut most likely maps to DL8.61.1.C (agreed consideration altered), and returns to D. A UAE tax advisor must confirm this. Resolve only proposes a code; a human confirms it.

Other risks: read-only ERP access within 30 days; receiving data that is too poor to use; Medius or SAP adding the same feature; Freehand entering the GCC (a partner or a threat). Each of these has a stop condition in the W4 readout.
